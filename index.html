How the motion is computed

Energy is conserved, so at every angle the speed is known (Eq. 7): θ̇/ω₀ = 2√(Er − (1 − cos θ)/2), with ω₀ = √(g/L). Time is the integral of the “pace” 1/θ̇ over angle (Eq. 8).

The time to the first turning point — a quarter of a swing, or half a turn — is the complete elliptic integral, computed exactly with Gauss’s arithmetic-geometric mean (Eqs. 10–11): ωosc/ω₀ = AGM(1, √(1 − Er)) and ωrot/ω₀ = 2√Er · AGM(1, √(1 − 1/Er)).

The times in between come from Simpson’s rule on the paper’s “Suite16” angles (fractions 1/8 ×7, then halving down to 1/2048, so the steps crowd in near the turning point). The dots on the chart are those 16 points. For a smooth animation each Suite16 step is split into 8 smaller Simpson steps, and the rest of each period is filled in by the turning-point symmetry of Table I. At exactly Er = 1 the page uses the separatrix θ = 2 arctan(sinh ω₀t): the pendulum creeps up to the top and never quite arrives.

Checked against a plain step-by-step (Runge–Kutta) integration of θ̈ = −ω₀² sin θ: the periods agree to better than one part in a million — see verify.mjs.
